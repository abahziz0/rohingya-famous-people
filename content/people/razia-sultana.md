A Rohingya lawyer, researcher and human rights activist born in Maungdaw, who practises law in Bangladesh with Rohingya refugees and documents the trauma, mass rape and trafficking suffered by Rohingya women during Myanmar's military operations. Her reports Witness to Horror (2017) and Rape by Command (2018) exposed the systematic use of sexual violence by Myanmar's security forces, and in 2019 she became the first Rohingya woman to receive the U.S. State Department's International Women of Courage Award.

## Biography

Razia Sultana is a prominent Rohingya lawyer, researcher, and human rights activist born in Maungdaw, Rakhine State, Myanmar. She currently practices law in Bangladesh, working directly with Rohingya refugees. Her work focuses on documenting trauma, mass rape, and human trafficking suffered by Rohingya women during Myanmar’s military operations.

Since 2014, Sultana has conducted extensive field research, interviewing hundreds of survivors and documenting their testimonies in influential reports like Witness to Horror (2017) and Rape by Command (2018), which exposed the systematic use of sexual violence by Myanmar security forces during the 2016–2017 "clearance operations."

She serves as Coordinator for Women and Children’s Affairs at the Free Rohingya Coalition (FRC) and founded the Rohingya Women Welfare Society (RWWS). For her tireless activism, she was honored with the International Women of Courage Award (2019) by the U.S. Department of State, becoming the first Rohingya woman to receive the award.
