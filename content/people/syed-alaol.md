A Bengali poet of the royal court of Arakan's Mrauk U kingdom, born in 1607 in Jalalpur, Bengal, and brought to Arakan in his youth after Portuguese pirates captured him. With the patronage of the prime minister Magan Thakur and the royal minister Syed Musa he wrote Padmavati (1648), a Bengali adaptation of Malik Muhammad Jayasi's Hindi epic, completed Daulat Qazi's unfinished Satimayna O Lorchandrani, and rendered Persian works of Nizami into Bengali, earning the title Pandit Kabi (Wise Poet).

## Biography

Syed Alaol was born in 1607 in Jalalpur, Fatehabad Pargana, Bengal Subah (now part of Madaripur District, Bangladesh). He hailed from an aristocratic family; his father served as a minister under the ruler of Fatehabad. Alaol was well-educated, mastering Bengali, Arabic, Persian, and Sanskrit, and was also trained in music and warfare .

In his youth, Alaol was captured by Portuguese pirates while traveling with his father and was taken to Arakan (Rakhine State, Myanmar). Initially serving as a bodyguard, he later gained recognition for his poetic talents and was appointed to the royal court of King Sanda Thudhamma of the Mrauk U dynasty. There, he received patronage from prominent figures such as Magan Thakur, the prime minister, and Syed Musa, the royal minister .

Alaol's literary contributions are significant. His most famous work, "Padmavati" (1648), is a Bengali adaptation of Malik Muhammad Jayasi's Hindi epic, blending Sufi themes with local folklore. Other notable works include:

- "Satimayna O Lorchandrani" (1659): Completed from Daulat Qazi's unfinished manuscript.

- "Saptapaykar" (1665): A Bengali rendition of Nizami Ganjavi's Persian text.

- "Saifulmuluk Badiuzzamal" (1669): A love story between a prince and a fairy.

- "Sikandarnama" (1671–72): A translation of Nizami's "Eskander-nama" .

Alaol's works are celebrated for their lyrical beauty and intellectual depth, earning him the title "Pandit Kabi" (Wise Poet). His legacy is honored in Bangladesh through the Alaol Sahitya Puroshkar, a literary award named in his honor .
